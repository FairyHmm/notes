> The graph below gives information from a report in 2010 about the use of energy in Australia since 1980 with projections until 2030

![Australian energy usage.jpg](/img/user/Resources/Ielts/Australian%20energy%20usage.jpg)

The line graph compares how energy consumption by five distinct fuel types differs, and the projection for future over a period of half a century starting from 1980 in Australia.

Overall, there is an upward trend for all energy types, and this trend will likely continue up to 2030. Also, oil takes the lead by a big margin for all of the periods shown.

To commence, in 1980, 35 billion units of oil were used to generate energy. Despite a drop to 30 billion units in 1990, the figure eventually recovered, and is expected to reach roughly 48 billion units by 2030. Natural gas and coal are the next two leading kinds, starting at 20 billion units and 16 billion units, respectively. Of which, nature gas has fluctuations in its usage, resulting in a forecast of 25 billion units by 2030. Meanwhile, coal is shown to have a more stable growth rate, surpassing natural gas in 2000, and is anticipated to climb ahead of natural gas by 7 billion units by 2030.

Nuclear and solar energy are not as widely used as the aforementioned fuels, both starting at 4 billion units each. They observed steady rises in their figures, with the former predicted to reach 8 billion units by 2030, while the latter is estimated to be consumed slightly less, at 6 billion units.
