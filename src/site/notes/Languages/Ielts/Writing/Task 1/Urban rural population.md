> The graphs show changes in the trends of the urban and rural population and by different age groups from 1950 to 2040.

![Urban rural population.png](/img/user/Languages/Ielts/.resources/Urban%20rural%20population.png)

Given are line graphs illustrating how data for population by place of living (city and countryside) and by seniority (15 or less, 16 to 65, and over 65), from 1950 to the present, with predictions up to 2040.

It is clear that as the time went on all figures rose, gradually or dramatically, and this trend is predicted to continue till the end of the research, bar one exception. Also, for each graph, urban residents and individual of the 16-to-65 age bracket stayed and will likely continue to stay consistently ahead of the following categories.

Upon a closer investigation for the first graph, at the start of the period, urban dwellers made up roughly 8 units, while rural citizens contributed by a factor of 4 less, resulting in a total of 10 units. Data for those living in metropolitan areas eventually passed the 15 and 20 units marks in 1980 and 2000, respectively, and is anticipated to hike to about 33 units by 2040. Whereas, the other category reported linear rises throughout the years, and will likely continue follow this trajectory, ending the period with 10 units.

As for the second graph, the three age groups, ordered by age from youngest to oldest, started at 4, 7, and 2 units, respectively. The 16 to 65 age group then went on a hike, having its figure be double and eventually triple of its initial state by the next two period marks. Meanwhile, the minors and seniors observed similar trajectories, only seeing grows of 3 units each at the turn of the century. By 2040, the middle-agers is forecast to see a less dramatic growth, finalising at 28 units. While the figure for the over 65 aged group is presumed to ascend to 9 units in 2040, the expectation for the youngest age bracket is the opposite, with a 2-unit dip.
