> The pie charts below show the main reasons why students chose to study at a particular UK University in 1987 and 2007.

![Reasons university.png](/img/user/Languages/Ielts/Writing/Task%201/Media/Reasons%20university.png)

Given are two pie charts comparing how British students in the years of 1987 and 2007 differed in their reasons for enrolling in a specific UK college.

Overall, the number of available courses seemed to be the main reason to choose a university in both years. Also, there had been a substantial hike in the proximity to parents respond in the latter year.

Suitable courses to get a degree was the main reason why students enrolled in the mentioned university, with 35% of the students stating this answer. This had largely stayed the same in 2007, which had witnessed a 2% uptick to the percentage. In 1987, only one in ten students chose to study at the university because of its distance to their families. Over the twenty-year period, this figure had more than doubled, resulting in the figure of 22%.

All other responds, namely resource quality, teaching quality, and sports and social activities, experienced drops in their data. For the former, in 1987, 21% of students chose this respond. It had then declined by 4% going into 2007. And as for the latter two, their figures experienced dramatic falls, finalising at 8% for excellence of teaching (from 15%) and only 6% for sports and social activities (from 19%).
