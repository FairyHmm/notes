> In the past, people stored knowledge in books. Nowadays, people store knowledge on the internet. Are there more advantages or disadvantages of this trend?

It is true that these days, information is often stored digitally instead of in books like it previously was. While this may have several drawbacks, I believe that the benefits are greater.

On one hand, these are some disadvantages of keeping knowledge online instead of the traditional way. Firstly, people with limited access to electricity and the internet, such as the Amish, would have trouble gathering information. In a lot of regions, books are sometimes the only viable method of retaining knowledge, due to the lack of resources. Secondly, it is easy to be distracted using the internet to access information. The internet is designed to catch our attention, so it could be difficult to stay focused when researching.

On the other hand, I believe that the benefits of storing information online are more significant. Primarily, knowledge can be easily stored, shared, replicated, and preserved. With the cloud technology, storing files is almost free of charge, and modern encryption and proxy technologies make it difficult to be censored or destroyed. The information is readily accessible at any moment and can also be shared with ease. In addition, knowledge can be found and accessed easily and efficiently. Modern search engines allow the retrieval of web pages containing information, or indexes of files on users’ systems. Artificial technology models, like the ChatGPT chatbot or DeepL translation service, make it easier to summarise information and translate foreign documents. As a result, learning productivity is greatly improved.

In conclusion, storing knowledge on the internet instead of in books certainly has its drawbacks, but I believe that the benefits outweigh them in the long run.
