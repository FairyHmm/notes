# Names
- Russians use three names:
    1. first name - имя
    2. middle/patronymic name - отчество
    3. last/family name - фамилия

## Details
- Women's last names add <c>-а</c> to the masculine form of the name.
- Miiddle name is formed from the father’s first name. Take the father’s first name and add an ending:
    - For men: <c>-ович</c> or <c>-(ь)евич</c> (<c>-й -ь</c>)
    > Борис - Борис<c>ович</c>/Борис<c>овна</c>
    - For women: <c>-овна</c> or <c>-(ь)евна</c> (<c>-й -ь</c>)
    > Юрий - Юрь<c>евич</c>/Юрь<c>евна</c>

- Russians use relatively few first names. There are only a dozen or so men's first names, and maybe three dozen women's first names. Creativity in baby-naming isn't encouraged.

## Formal
- To address a Russian formally, don't use "Mr." or "Ms." Instead, address the person using his or her first name and middle name, and sometimes last name.
    > Богданов Александр Борисович

    > Богданова Александра Борисовна

## Informal
- You can choose between different ways of addressing people in informal situations.
- The middle name can also be used here to show respect towards the person you with whom you are speaking (e.g. an older person or teacher, a boss).
- Russians also use diminutives or nicknames. Each name typically has a version used by:
    - your best friend
    - your other friends
    - your teachers
    - your grandmother
    - you're scolded
    - etc
> Александр - Саша

> Иван - Ваня
