# Pronounciation
## Hard, soft con

- In Russian, con have two sounds: a hard and a soft one.
- You should certainly pay attention to this, as the meaning of some words also changes depending on the sound.


- How can you distinguish between a hard and a soft sound?
    - If there’s a <c>Ь Е Ё И Ю Я</c> after a con, it becomes softer. For example:

    ||||
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| <c>н</c>ос<br><c>н</c>ёс | [<c>n̪</c>o̞s̪]<br>[<c>n</c>jos] | "a nose"<br>"was carrying" |
| <c>л</c>ук<br><c>л</c>юк | [<c>l</c>uk]<br>[<c>l</c>ʉk] | "an onion"<br>"a hatch" |


    - <c>Ж Ш Ц</c> always have a hard sound. When followed by soft vow <c>И Е</c>, they are pronounced as <c>Ы Э</c>. For example:

    ||||
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| д<c>ж</c>инсы | [ˈd<c>ʐ</c>ɨnsɨ] | "jeans" |
| <c>ц</c>ентр | [<c>ʦ</c>ɛntr] | "a centre" |
| <c>ж</c>ест | [<c>ʐ</c>ɛst] | "a gesture" |

## Intonation

- Intonation plays a major role in spoken Russian language.
- Luckily, this difficult topic only has five basic rules:

1. In declarative sentences the tone is even with a slight fall at the end.
> Мы живём в Минске. "We live in Minsk."

2. In case of questions starting with a question word, the intonation rises with the question word, drops after it, and remains even for the rest of the phrase.
> Где ты живёшь? "Where do you live?"

3. In case of questions without a question word, the intonation rises on the word in question, drops after it, and remains even for the rest of the phrase.
> Мы идём в <u>кино</u>? "Are we going to the cinema? (or somewhere else?)"
> 
> Мы <u>идём</u> в кино? "Are we going to the cinema? (or are we doing anything else?)"

4. In incomplete questions the intonation ascends and then descends like a wave.
> Мы живём в Минске. А вы? "We live in Minsk. What about you?"

5. In exclamations the intonation rises at the beginning, levels off, and remains high. It drops at the end of the sentence.
> Какой прекрасный день! "What a beautiful day!"

## Stress
- Word stress is a tricky part of the Russian language,  an incorrect stress can lead to a change in meaning and misunderstandings.
- The worst part is that Russians don’t mark the stress in writing, so you are on your own here! Our advice: you should check the stress for every new word you learn.

### Stressed and unstressed vow

- Stressed vow are pronounced more clearly, strongly, and longer than unstressed ones.
- Unstressed <c>О</c>, <c>Е</c>, and <c>Я</c> change pronunciation:

|||
| :---: | :---: |
| <c>O</c> → <c>A</c> | <c>о</c>н [<c>o̞</c>n] |
| ^^ | <c>о</c>на [<c>ɐˈ</c>na] |
| <c>Е</c> → <c>И</c> | л<c>е</c>с [l<c>ʲe</c>s] |
| ^^ | л<c>е</c>са [l<c>ʲɪˈ</c>sa] |
| <c>Я</c> → <c>ЙИ</c> | <c>я</c> [<c>ja</c>] |
| ^^ | Япони<c>я</c> [jɪˈponʲɪ<c>jə</c>] |

### Stable and moving stress

- It’s almost impossible to guess where the stress should go.
- The stress can be both stable and moving in Russian.
    - The stable stress doesn’t change place while being declined or conjugated:
        - Sg: телеф<c>о</c>н, телеф<c>о</c>на, ... "a telephone"
        - Pl: телеф<c>о</c>ны, телеф<c>о</c>нов, ... "telephones"
    - The moving stress changes its place depending on the form of the word:
        - Sg: рук<c>а</c>, р<c>у</c>ку, рук<c>и</c>, ... "a hand"
        - Pl: р<c>у</c>ки, рук, рук<c>а</c>х, ... "hands"
