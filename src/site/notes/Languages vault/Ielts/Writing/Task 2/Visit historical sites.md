These days, there is a growing trend of going to historical attractions and museums alike in various nations on the globe. While this is popular for visitors, those living in the area do not do this often. An array of justification could be used to explain the given phenomenon, and several solutions may be utilised to pursue local residents to do this.

To commence, there are several explanations for the tendency. For one, while tourists want to explore the cultural and historical happenstances of a place, residents of such an area are already educated about those information, so visiting the sites is just repeating what they already know. As such, while outsiders find these areas fascinating, local dwellers may think of such locations as dull, common occurrences. For another, as museums and historical sites are built to attract guests, and to make profits, the price markups could be overwhelming for the residents, who only want to learn more about the land they are living in. Combined with the fact that most of the knowledge is known by most people in the area, it further disincentives local people to come.

Regarding how the situation could be improved, a multitude of solutions may be used, such as to make these attractions free and more accessible, or to organise tours to such places. With regards to the former, as these locations offer cultural and historical knowledge about a particular piece of land, outsiders are willing to go there and learn about them. By providing interesting, uncommon information about the place, as well as persuading tourists to support, a donation-based business model could be sustainable. This not only allows more individuals to come, residents may also come here in their free time without worrying about the price. As for the later, regional history always fascinates school children. Tours to historical sites alike are good, for students to have great experiences learning about their homeland, as to help them appreciate and be proud of what came before.

To summarise, a variety of explanations may be used for the tendency, being why visitors and not residents like to go to historical sites. Many solutions could be used, including changing how these places are operated as a whole, and to make people more interested.
