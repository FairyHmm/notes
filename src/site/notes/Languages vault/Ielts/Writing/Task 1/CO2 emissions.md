The line graph gives information about the amount of carbon dioxide emitted per person in four different nations from 1967 to 2007.

It is clear that the UK released the highest amount of CO2 per person over the research period. Also, the UK and Sweden witnessed drops their carbon dioxide emissions, while Italy and Portugal saw rises in their figures.

In 1967, about 11 metric tons of carbon dioxide were emitted by each UK citizen. Sweden emitted about 9 metric tons of CO2 per person, compared to Italy which released only 4.5 metric tons each person. Portugal had the smallest CO2 emissions, with their figure being at only about 1 metric ton each person.

In 2007, the UK's emission rate gradually declined to just under 9 tons of CO2 per person. The carbon dioxide emissions of Sweden peaked at 10.5 metric tons in 1977, then rapidly went down to 5.5 tons in 2007. In 2007, each Italian released 3.5 more tons of CO2 than the figure in 1967. Portugal witnessed their emission rate dramatically rose to 5.5 metric tons of carbon dioxide per person in the same year.
