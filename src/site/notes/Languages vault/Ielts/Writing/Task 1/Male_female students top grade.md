> The charts below show the percentages of male and female students getting top grades in 1960 and 2000

![18b7d70512ff4d4bb7be9ec8ad70923f.png](/img/user/resources/18b7d70512ff4d4bb7be9ec8ad70923f.png)

Given are graphs delineating the proportions of students getting top scores across five subjects, namely science, arts, maths, languages, and humanities, of both genders in the years of 1960 and 2000.

It is clear that male students were better at science and maths than female one, as well as the reverse. Also, there were more variations between the percentages for boys over the years than their counterparts.

Upon closer investigation for the first chart, the changes of figures between the years were significant, of up to 23% for the humanities courses (from 22% to 45%). Arts observed a similar trend, though to a lesser degree of one tenth. Science and maths experienced an opposite tenancy, with the percentage of male pupils receiving top grades declined by 15% each. The only exception were the languages courses, which remained stable at just over 20%.

With regards to the other graph, there were less fluctuations over the years. Arts, languages, and humanities were clearly more popular than the rest, though, their figures were shrinking (between 5% and 10%). Maths had its data stayed at 5%, while Science saw a minor upsurge of only 1% (from 10%).
