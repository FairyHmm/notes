The line graph compares changes in the number of cars owned by British families over a period of 37 years.

Overall, car ownership in British households ascended during the research period. In particular, the proportion of households with two or more cars upsurged, while the figure for families without a car shrank.

In 1971, nearly half of British families did not own a vehicle. Around 45% of households owned a car, whereas the figure for families having two cars was less than one tenth. Households with three or more cars were uncommon, with only 1% of families falling into this category.

Households with one car became the most common type in Britain, with the figure stayed stable at about 45%. In 2007, the percentage of families without a car plunged steadily by about 25%. By contrast, the figures for households with two or more cars gradually hiked. The percentage of families with two cars steadily soared to over a quarter. Similarly, households with three or more cars also almost accounted for 10% of the total.
